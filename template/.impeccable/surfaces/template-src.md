# Surface: the one-page restaurant site (template/src)

Scope: Persuade. Audience: the owner on a phone first, their customers second. Job: recognise the place in one viewport, read what they cook and when, tap call or WhatsApp. Proof on hand: name, up to five Google photos, rating, hours, Claude-drafted menu. Constraints: zero client JS, per-client theme tokens, any name length, zero to five photos, Albanian copy from config only.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The template is a skeleton, not a look. Its one idea is that every restaurant gets its own visual world, derived from its own photos, on a structure that never changes: name filling the phone on a field, the photo beside it, a menu read as a menu, hours and contact, the call and WhatsApp bar locked below. It refuses the category default of one house style stamped on every client (the kilim it replaced was exactly that), and it refuses the other default too: a white page with a photo and a serif name that could be anyone's.

OWN-WORLD: The skeleton owns no colour, face, corner or edge. Each client's config carries bg, ink, primary, accent, muted, display, body, displayWeight, radius, frame, generated by factory/theme.mjs from the restaurant's photos, name, category and reviews, then repaired for contrast. The skeleton draws: a primary field top and bottom, patches in muted (the largest menu group in primary) edged per frame and rounded per radius, hairlines in currentColor, one accent-filled control per surface, tabular figures in a fixed price column. With every word removed a page still shows its client's palette, corner and edge, and nothing of the template's.

STORY: The owner opens the link and sees their own room's colours and their own photo under their name, and reads it as "someone made this for us". A customer learns what is cooked, that it is open now, and taps WhatsApp.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Phone 390 wide. The name in the display face at the theme weight filling the width on the primary field, the headline beneath in the body face. The photo as a patch in the theme's corner and edge; without a photo, the name's initial in the display face, accent on muted. Along the field's bottom the hours fringe in tabular figures. Locked at the bottom, the two-button bar: "Thirr" plain on the ground, WhatsApp in the accent. Desktop 1280 and up: the field splits, name, headline, CTAs and hours left, photo patch right. The hero variant changes the shape, never the contents: `split` lets the photo lead edge to edge (top of the phone, right half of the desktop field); `type` drops the photo and sets the name as large as the wrap allows.

FORM: The skeleton with a generated world per client. No direction roll was run: the direction is that the template holds no direction of its own, and each client's world is decided by the generator from evidence (photos), not by taste. Signature interaction: the rise. On load the hero words and photo settle into place, 500ms exponential ease-out, once; nothing else animates on load. Menu row rules turn accent on hover in 160ms; buttons press to scale 0.97.

Raises kept from the previous world: the name sized from its longest word; one highlighted menu group; one tabular figure at a fixed column; gallery repacks in whole cells; the locked anchor bar; one reading axis on phones.

VARIANTS: `theme.layout` picks one shape per section, chosen by the theme call from the archetype and the photos, not by the template: hero photo | split | type, menu list | cards | board, gallery grid | strip | single | none, rhythm tight | airy, order menu-first | about-first. Every combination builds; each is CSS and an Astro conditional, still no script. Beyond the sections above the page now carries: testimonials from Google reviews (first names only) after the story, a "shembull" tag on menu items the generator invented, and Google's author credit with "Powered by Google" under every Google photo, nothing under Instagram or owner photos. Fixtures under `places/_fixtures/` hold one config per combination worth looking at.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
